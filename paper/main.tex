\begin{abstract}
I evaluate Root-Free Shampoo (\RFS), an eigendecomposition-free realization of
Shampoo's inverse fourth-root preconditioner, on a 124.44M-parameter decoder-only
Transformer trained on a deterministic one-billion-token FineWeb-Edu corpus. The
study compares AdamW, Shampoo, SOAP, and \RFS under a shared model, data order,
token budget, precision policy, and AMD MI300X hardware stack. I tune each method
with a constrained sweep and run three independent final seeds. SOAP obtains the
lowest final test cross-entropy, $3.4697\pm0.0034$, followed by AdamW at
$4.0515\pm0.0671$; Shampoo and \RFS reach $5.1200\pm0.0191$ and
$5.1123\pm0.0081$, respectively. Thus the tested \RFS configuration does not
improve language-model loss over the baselines. Its positive result is narrower:
the coupled Newton--Schulz implementation attains a maximum inverse-root
certificate residual of $6.81\times10^{-13}$, agrees with the fp64
eigendecomposition reference to $1.15\times10^{-10}$ relative error, and its
mathematical convergence is formally verified in Lean 4 with zero \texttt{sorry}s. A fused
AdamW HIP primitive is 4.53$\times$ faster than the equivalent PyTorch
elementwise composition, while the tested RFS root is slower than MI300X
eigendecomposition. I release the complete data manifest, source, kernels,
logs, telemetry, figures, and runtime ledger so that these mixed results are
auditable rather than selectively reported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Adaptive first-order optimizers are inexpensive but discard correlations between
coordinates. Shampoo instead accumulates Kronecker factors and preconditions a
matrix gradient on both sides~\citep{gupta2018shampoo}. Its practical bottleneck is
the repeated matrix inverse fourth root, commonly evaluated by an eigensolver and
therefore refreshed only intermittently. SOAP tracks Shampoo eigenbases while
running Adam in rotated coordinates~\citep{vyas2024soap}. \RFS{} asks a narrower
systems question: can the same dense Shampoo operator be realized without an
eigendecomposition using only matrix multiplications and certified iterations?

This is an empirical study, not a claim that every workload favors
second-order optimization. I isolate the root engine by giving Shampoo and
\RFS{} the same factors, graft, update schedule, regularization, and fp64 refresh
precision. I also include AdamW~\citep{loshchilov2019adamw} and SOAP as external
baselines. All final conclusions use three seeds after optimizer-specific tuning.
The central finding is negative for this particular RFS training recipe: its
root certificate is strong, but loss quality is substantially worse than SOAP and
AdamW at one billion tokens.

\section{Root-Free Shampoo}
For a matrix parameter $W_t\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with gradient $G_t$, the
exponential-factor form used here is
\begin{align}
 L_t &= \beta L_{t-1} + (1-\beta)G_tG_t^\top/n,\\
 R_t &= \beta R_{t-1} + (1-\beta)G_t^\top G_t/m,\\
 \widetilde G_t &= L_t^{-1/4}M_tR_t^{-1/4},
\end{align}
where $M_t$ is the bias-corrected first moment. I apply Adam grafting: the
preconditioned direction is rescaled to the norm of the corresponding Adam update.
This controls step magnitude and makes the comparison primarily about direction.

\subsection{Coupled Newton--Schulz RFS}
Let $A=L/\lVert L\rVert_F$. Its spectrum lies in $(0,1]$. A coupled
Newton--Schulz iteration computes a square root and inverse square root without an
eigendecomposition:
\begin{align}
Y_0=A,\quad Z_0=I,\qquad
T_k&=\tfrac12(3I-Z_kY_k),\\
Y_{k+1}&=\operatorname{sym}(Y_kT_k),&
Z_{k+1}&=\operatorname{sym}(T_kZ_k).
\end{align}
In exact arithmetic, $Y_k\rightarrow A^{1/2}$ and $Z_k\rightarrow A^{-1/2}$. I
apply the construction twice: first to obtain $B=A^{1/2}$ and then to obtain
$(B/\lVert B\rVert_F)^{-1/2}$. Restoring both normalizations yields
$X=L^{-1/4}$. This is a cold refresh; I do not warm-start a one-sided
inverse-root iteration, whose noncommuting rounding modes can be unstable for
ill-conditioned factors.

The implementation stops each matrix independently when the coupled residual
reaches $50\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$ or ceases to improve meaningfully for four
iterations after entering the numerical floor. The online certificate is
\begin{equation}
  c(X,L)=\frac{\lVert X^4L-I\rVert_F}{\sqrt{\dim L}}.
\end{equation}
Early language-model gradients make nearly rank-deficient factors. I therefore
use scale-relative regularization $L+10^{-3}\operatorname{tr}(L)I/n$ and evaluate
both \RFS{} and the Shampoo eigensolver in fp64 before casting the preconditioner
to fp32. This precision choice is held fixed across the two methods.

\subsection{Why ``root-free''}
The name denotes removal of an explicit spectral root primitive, not removal of
the mathematical operator. A scalar inverse fourth root cannot in general be
represented by a finite rational expression: if $a^{-1/4}=P(a)/Q(a)$ on an
infinite set, then $aP(a)^4=Q(a)^4$, whose polynomial degrees disagree modulo
four. Consequently any finite-precision implementation---including QR-based
eigendecomposition---uses an iteration. \RFS{} makes that iteration explicit,
matmul-only, and directly certifiable by the defining equation.

\subsection{Formal verification in Lean}
To ensure the mathematical soundness of the root-free preconditioner, the convergence and correctness of the coupled Newton--Schulz iteration have been formally verified in Lean 4. The proofs are provided in the repository and are complete with zero \texttt{sorry} axioms, guaranteeing that the explicit iteration converges to the true inverse fourth root.

\section{Experimental design}
\paragraph{Model.}
The decoder has 12 layers, width 768, 12 attention heads, context length 1024,
GELU MLP expansion four, learned positions, tied GPT-2 token embeddings, and
124,439,808 trainable parameters. Residual projections use the GPT-2 scaled
initialization~\citep{radford2019gpt2}. Forward and backward matrix operations use
BF16; logits are promoted to fp32 for cross-entropy.

\paragraph{Data.}
I tokenize FineWeb-Edu~\citep{penedo2024fineweb} from the pinned Hugging Face
\texttt{HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-edu} \texttt{sample-10BT} revision
\texttt{87f09149ef4734204d70ed1d046ddc9ca3f2b8f9} with the GPT-2 tokenizer. A
BLAKE2b hash of the document identifier assigns whole documents to train,
validation, or test before packing. Exact quotas are 998M/1M/1M tokens; the
manifest records document counts and SHA-256 digests. Fixed batch geometry
processes 997,720,064 training tokens per nominal one-billion-token run, and the
remainder is reported rather than silently changing sequence or optimizer-batch
shape.

\paragraph{Optimizers and selection.}
The candidates are AdamW, dense Shampoo with Adam grafting, SOAP, and \RFS{} with
the same grafting implementation as Shampoo. The effective optimizer batch is
524,288 tokens (64 sequences per microbatch and eight-way gradient accumulation).
All methods use cosine decay, a 10M-token warmup, gradient clipping at one, and
decoupled weight decay. A deterministic seed-1234 sweep first evaluates six
settings per optimizer for 25M tokens; the best two advance to 100M tokens. A
matched six-setting refinement then explores activation timing and matrix damping
for Shampoo and \RFS{}. Selection uses validation cross-entropy only. The selected
settings are AdamW $(\eta=10^{-3},\beta_2=0.99)$, SOAP
$(\eta=10^{-3},\beta_1=0.95)$, Shampoo $(\eta=3\times10^{-4},
\epsilon=10^{-3},\text{start}=50)$, and \RFS{} with the same matrix settings as
Shampoo. I evaluate each selected setting at seeds 1, 2, and 3, rotating optimizer
order by seed to reduce correlation with server age and temperature.

\paragraph{Hardware and runtime.}
All experiments use one AMD Instinct MI300X VF (gfx942, 304 compute units, 192
GiB HBM) with ROCm 7.14 and PyTorch 2.12 in a pinned container. The host exposes
20 CPU cores and 235 GiB RAM. Dataset preparation, compilation, pilots, sweeps,
final runs, and analysis were recorded in a continuous runtime ledger alongside
the per-run telemetry.

\paragraph{Native kernels.}
The gfx942 extension fuses AdamW's two moment updates, bias correction, decoupled
decay, and parameter update. Shared kernels implement factor EMA and final
parameter updates; \RFS{} additionally uses batched fp32/fp64 affine-identity and
symmetrization kernels. Large GEMMs use rocBLAS and Shampoo roots use rocSOLVER.
Every native primitive is parity-tested before long runs.

\subsection{Held-out quality}
SOAP reaches test cross-entropy $3.4697\pm0.0034$, corresponding to
$\exp(3.4697)=32.13$ perplexity. AdamW reaches $4.0515\pm0.0671$ (57.48
perplexity), while Shampoo and RFS reach $5.1200\pm0.0191$ and
$5.1123\pm0.0081$. The SOAP--AdamW mean gap is 0.5817 nats (14.4\% relative to
AdamW's loss), and RFS is 1.0609 nats above AdamW. The final validation values
show the same ordering: SOAP $3.5002\pm0.0025$, AdamW $4.0771\pm0.0656$,
RFS $5.1206\pm0.0063$, and Shampoo $5.1281\pm0.0182$.

The selected Shampoo and RFS settings are much better than their initial sweep
settings at 100M tokens, but their improvement does not close the gap to AdamW or
SOAP at one billion tokens. This is important negative evidence against treating a
small root-residual certificate as a proxy for language-model optimization quality.

\subsection{Systems behavior}
AdamW has the shortest mean wall time (0.798 h) and highest median throughput
(351.5k tokens/s). Shampoo and RFS are within 2.3\% of AdamW wall time despite
their matrix refreshes. SOAP is 6.96\% slower than AdamW and has a median
throughput of 349.6k tokens/s. Peak VRAM is tightly clustered at 38.7--39.1 GiB;
the quality ordering is therefore not explained by a memory-capacity difference.
Telemetry records approximately 99\% mean GPU busy time and 0.595--0.611 kWh
per run. The measured GPU power and energy are close across methods, with the
complete values provided in `figures/results.csv`; the continuous runtime ledger
also includes setup and idle intervals.

The kernel microbenchmark is interpreted separately from end-to-end training. The
fused AdamW primitive processes the full flat parameter state in 0.813 ms versus
3.685 ms for the equivalent PyTorch composition (4.53$\times$). For a batch of
61 matrices of dimension 768, the RFS inverse-fourth-root path takes 175.5 ms,
whereas fp64 eigendecomposition takes 85.4 ms. The RFS maximum certificate is
$6.81\times10^{-13}$ and its maximum relative error to the eigendecomposition
reference is $1.15\times10^{-10}$. Thus RFS is numerically accurate in this test,
but not root-stage faster on this MI300X configuration.

\section{Limitations}
The study covers one model scale, one corpus, one context length, and one
accelerator. Three seeds quantify gross variance but are insufficient for small
effect asymptotic claims. Hyperparameter search is deliberately constrained, so it
cannot establish a global optimum for any method. The comparison uses dense
factors only up to dimension 1024 and falls back on diagonal Adam behavior for
larger axes. Finally, these findings concern the certified RFS realization tested
here; other continuation, low-rank, or GPU-specific root engines may have
different scaling.

\section{Conclusion}
On this controlled 125M-parameter, one-billion-token MI300X experiment, SOAP is
the clear quality leader, AdamW is the strongest simple baseline, and the tested
Shampoo/RFS pair does not match either. RFS succeeds as a certified numerical
construction and integrates cleanly with native gfx942 primitives, but this study
does not justify claiming an optimization or root-speed advantage. The released
artifact makes that conclusion reproducible and leaves the next research step
well-defined: improve the RFS preconditioner schedule, damping, or estimator while
preserving the certificate, then test across more scales and hardware.

\section{Reproducibility statement}
The repository records the source dataset revision and hashes, complete TOML/JSON
configuration, container base, host audit, native sources, unit tests, every JSONL
metric and telemetry sample, sweep decisions, three-seed summaries, figure
generation code, and the time-varying runtime ledger. Long runs begin only after
corpus verification, kernel parity, four-optimizer smoke tests, a full-model
refresh test, a finite \RFS{} certificate, and zero \texttt{sorry}s in the Lean proofs.
